Privacy policy

Dateline Atlas for Android and iPhone · effective October 14, 2026

The short version. The app has no accounts and no analytics of its own, and its publisher doesn't receive or sell your personal data. The free version shows one banner ad from Google AdMob, and Google collects data to show and measure it. Weather is optional: if you turn it on, your phone's approximate location goes to the US National Weather Service to look up the forecast, and nowhere else.

News

The app downloads the day's story feed, a public file, from GitHub Pages (github.io), which hosts it. Like any website, GitHub receives your IP address and basic device information when the file is downloaded; see GitHub's privacy statement. The app keeps a copy of the feed on your phone so it opens offline. Stories link to the outlets that published them; when you open one of those links, that outlet's own privacy policy applies.

Weather and your location

Weather is off until you turn it on, and your phone asks before sharing its location. You can say no and the app works the same, without weather. If you turn it on, the app reads your approximate location (about a town's accuracy) and sends it to the US National Weather Service (api.weather.gov) to get your forecast. The location is saved only on your phone, to show the forecast next time, and you can turn weather off at any time in the app or in your phone's settings. The publisher never receives it.

Ads in the free version

The free version shows one banner ad at the bottom of the screen, served by Google AdMob. To choose, show and measure ads, and to prevent fraud, Google's software in the app collects data such as your device's advertising identifier, IP address, approximate location derived from the IP address, device and app information, and how ads are viewed and tapped. See how Google uses information from apps that use its services and Google's privacy policy.

  • Where the law requires it (for example in the European Economic Area, the UK and Switzerland, and some US states), the app asks for your consent first, using Google's consent tool. You can change your choice later from "Ad privacy choices" on the app's Sources tab.
  • On iPhone, the app asks whether it may track you across other companies' apps and websites. If you say no, ads aren't personalized with your advertising identifier.
  • On Android, you can reset or delete your advertising ID in your phone's settings (Settings › Privacy › Ads, or Google › Ads).
  • The Upgrade removes the ad, and Google's ad software then stops loading ads.

The Upgrade (in-app purchase)

The Upgrade is bought through Google Play or the Apple App Store, which handle the payment under their own terms and privacy policies. The publisher doesn't receive your payment details. The app asks the store whether your account owns the Upgrade and remembers the answer on your phone.
